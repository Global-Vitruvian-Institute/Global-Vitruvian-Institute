# World data

Charts about the world are easier to look at than to picture. These come from [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/), a research project that publishes its data and its sources openly.

The charts load with this page, so opening it fetches them from ourworldindata.org. That request goes to their servers, as any website visit would; nothing you have written here is part of it. You can also follow the link under any chart to read it on their site.

## Population

> [!chart] How many people live in the world
> The United Nations estimates the population of every country each year. This chart compares a few of the largest countries; switch between the map and the chart to see how they have changed since 1950.
> https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-and-demography?tab=map&facet=none&country=CHN~IND~USA~IDN~PAK&indicator=population&sex=all&age=all&variant=estimates&hideControls=false

## Life expectancy

> [!chart] How long people live, on average
> Period life expectancy is the number of years someone born in a given year would live if death rates stayed as they were then. Compare countries and regions, or follow the world average over time.
> https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/life-expectancy

## Mental health

> [!chart] How common mental illness is
> The estimated share of people living with each condition in a given year, whether or not they were diagnosed. The estimates come from surveys, medical records and statistical models, so they describe a population rather than any one person.
> https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/mental-illnesses-prevalence

## Schooling

> [!chart] How many years of school people get
> Average years of formal education for people aged 15 to 64, going back to 1870 for the countries with the earliest records. Follow one country and you can see how much schooling changed within a few generations.
> https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/mean-years-of-schooling-long-run

## Money

> [!chart] How many people live in extreme poverty
> The share of people living below the International Poverty Line, which this chart puts at $1.90 a day, adjusted for inflation and for differences in living costs between countries. Its estimates reach back to 1820.
> https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-of-population-living-in-extreme-poverty

## Time with other people

> [!chart] Who Americans spend their time with, by age
> Hours per day spent with friends, family, children, and alone, averaged from surveys in the United States between 2010 and 2024. Look at how the hours with friends change between the teenage years and middle age.
> https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/time-spent-with-relationships-by-age-us
